Hero workflows first • survey-led plan • product + UX + implementation

From portal catalog to workflow workbench

A concentrated plan for turning the GenAI portal into the place where high-value work starts, gets guided, gets reviewed, and becomes repeatable. The recommendation is to make a small number of workflows excellent first, then make those flows safely agentic, then expand the model.

424 survey responses across 6 CoEs Current estate combines a modern portal and legacy SharePoint assets Top barrier: client data restrictions Dominant demand: content, research, insight, client-ready deliverables First concentration bet: 4 hero workflows

Why this route is the strongest one

Trying to improve everything at once would produce a better portal surface but not a better default way of working. The survey signals point to a smaller number of recurring tasks, while the screenshots show the current portal already has seeds for some of them. That is the right setup for a workflow-first move: use what already exists, concentrate quality, and let the product learn from a small number of carefully instrumented flows.

Core recommendation: make four workflows excellent first — proposal / RFP response, research / competitive synthesis, meeting notes → actions / PMO follow-through, and executive brief / deck narrative. Keep requirements / spec / test pack as the next expansion flow once the workflow framework is working.

What this plan deliberately avoids

  • It does not assume a new enterprise AI platform must be procured before progress can happen.
  • It does not start with a broad free-form agent for every task in the portal.
  • It does not redesign every legacy page before the new model proves itself.
  • It does not treat awareness campaigns as the main solution to a routing and execution problem.

Executive brief

The portal is already more than a library, but it is not yet the default operating layer for AI-assisted work. The best route forward is to stop expanding breadth first and instead turn a small number of workflows into the standard, trusted, repeatable entry point for real work.

What the portal is today
Discovery + enablement

It already offers search, role/BU context, tools, patterns, prompts, playbooks, and learning continuity. It is strong at helping people find things.

What is missing
Guided execution

Users still do too much assembly themselves: picking the right content type, combining tools, and carrying work across pages.

What to build next
4 hero workflows

Concentrate on four reusable flows, make them excellent, instrument them deeply, and use them to establish the product pattern.

What comes after
Bounded agentic steps

Only once the flows are stable should the portal automate parts of them — with explicit review, state visibility, and auditability.

Why workflow-first is the right route

  • The surveys show strong recurring demand for document generation, research, insights, and client-facing outputs — not just generic curiosity.
  • The screenshots show the portal already has visible seeds for several of these flows, which makes the first slice realistic.
  • The dominant barriers are data restrictions, trust, and unclear use-case fit. Those are better solved by curated, bounded flows than by a general agent layer.
  • Each hero workflow becomes a reusable product unit: discoverable, measurable, improvable, and later automatable.

Design rules for the first concentration bet

  • Workflow before page: the portal should answer “how do I do this work?” before “which page do you want?”
  • Boundaries before automation: client-restricted and internal-safe contexts must route differently.
  • Review before send: anything executive- or client-facing requires explicit review design.
  • Config before custom: workflow definitions and asset mappings should live in a registry, not in hard-coded page branches.
  • Starts, completions, repeats over page views: success is workflow adoption, not browsing.
Call to action for leadership: sponsor the workflow model, not another browsing layer. Approve four hero flows, name owners, allow stale-content retirement, and treat the first build slice as the product pattern for everything that follows.

Current estate

Core current-state problem

The portal already understands content categories. It does not yet understand the user’s task strongly enough. That is why the next leap should not be “more pages” or “more search” in the abstract. It should be task routing + executable workflows + visible trust.

Plain language: right now the product is closer to a very good AI directory. It needs to become a workbench.

The four hero workflows to back now

  1. Proposal / RFP response — highest strategic leverage with the strongest existing seed assets.
  2. Research / competitive synthesis — broadest cross-CoE demand and easiest to improve without deep integration.
  3. Meeting notes → actions / PMO follow-through — smallest high-value habit-forming flow.
  4. Executive brief / deck narrative — direct path to client-ready output and already visible in the home page.

Why not pick more right away

  • Because a workflow-first strategy only works if the first flows are clearly better than today’s browse-and-assemble behavior.
  • Because the selected flows already span the survey’s biggest work modes: research, drafting, synthesis, summary, actioning, and deliverable creation.
  • Because requirements/spec packs and broader data-to-insight flows become much cheaper to add after the workflow shell, trust model, and telemetry model exist.
Key discipline: do not treat “hero workflows” as a prioritization slogan. Treat them as a product concentration rule.

State progression

The portal should not jump straight from catalog to autonomous agent. It should move through a sequence where each state solves a specific problem and leaves behind reusable product primitives for the next state.

State 0 — Catalog portal

What changes: Search, catalogs, patterns, prompts, playbooks, training, and legacy SharePoint surfaces exist, but the user still assembles the workflow manually.

User experience: Strong discovery, weak execution continuity. Patterns explain; they do not yet run. Search finds; it does not yet route decisively.

Product / tech shift: Content lives across the new portal and legacy sources. Role and BU context exist, but workflow state and workflow registry are not yet obvious product primitives.

State 1 — Hero workflow launchers

What changes: A small set of hero workflows become first-class entry points with task intake, best-next-action results, trust panels, and save/resume.

User experience: The portal stops asking “which content type do you want?” and starts asking “what are you trying to get done?”

Product / tech shift: Add workflow definitions, asset mappings, launch adapters, workflow instance state, and event telemetry. Keep ranking mostly rule-based and curated.

State 2 — Guided workbench

What changes: Hero workflows gain workspace shells, structured steps, reusable outputs, reviewer handoff, and contextual micro-guidance.

User experience: The portal becomes part of the work, not just the step before the work. My Space becomes a true “resume work” surface.

Product / tech shift: Workflow instances, step completion, output presets, review gates, and stronger search/result-grouping are now core objects.

State 3 — Bounded agentic execution

What changes: Inside the hero workflows only, the system can take bounded actions: propose a plan, chain approved tools, perform safe synthesis, and stop for review before sensitive steps.

User experience: Users see visible status, what the agent is doing, which tools are involved, and when a human must approve or edit.

Product / tech shift: Planner, tool adapters, audit trail, approval gates, fallback handling, and evaluation checks are introduced — but only for curated flows.

State 4 — Expansion platform

What changes: The workflow framework expands to the next candidates such as requirements/spec packs and data-to-insight narratives, without redesigning the product from scratch.

User experience: New flows feel native because they inherit the same task intake, trust panel, workflow shell, and telemetry pattern.

Product / tech shift: Workflow authoring, stronger ranking, richer adapters, and deeper analytics make expansion cheaper and safer.

What should actually be built first

The realistic first build slice is State 1 plus the most important parts of State 2: hero workflow launchers, grouped task routing, save/resume, trust panels, review gates, and telemetry. That is enough to materially change adoption without overcommitting the codebase.

What should wait until the shell is stable

System-wide agent autonomy, write-back into external systems, deep cross-system orchestration, and platform-procurement-dependent designs should wait until the hero workflows are demonstrably working and trusted.

Build slice: what to implement in the first concentration bet

This tab is the practical blueprint. It is deliberately scoped to what is realistic when the goal is to produce a visible shift in user experience without rewriting the whole platform.

Recommended build order

Implement in this sequence

  1. Define the workflow registry and asset mappings.
  2. Add the task-intake router and grouped result response.
  3. Create the shared workflow workspace shell.
  4. Add trust panels and review gates.
  5. Persist save/resume state and surface it in My Space.
  6. Instrument telemetry and feedback pulses.
  7. Release the four hero workflows inside the same shell.
What makes this realistic

Why this sequence is manageable

  • It reuses the current portal shell rather than replacing it.
  • It depends mainly on existing content and tool surfaces.
  • It keeps ranking curated and inspectable instead of overbuilding a generalized AI planner.
  • It creates reusable primitives that every next workflow can inherit.

Workstream 1 — Workflow registry and asset mapping

  • Create one machine-readable record per hero workflow: name, intent phrases, steps, outputs, trust rules, linked tools, linked prompts, linked examples, owner, last validated.
  • Map every hero workflow to its current portal assets and identify gaps.
  • Keep this config-driven so adding the next workflow is mostly metadata + content, not a full code branch.

Workstream 2 — Task router and result grouping

  • Convert the homepage entry into a task-intake surface that routes to one recommended workflow first.
  • Group search results by action type: recommended workflow, tools, prompts, examples, training.
  • Use curated rules first; avoid overbuilding a full agentic search layer before the flows are stable.

Workstream 3 — Workflow workspace shell

  • Add a shared workspace layout with input panel, stepper, linked assets, output tabs, trust panel, and save/resume.
  • Persist workflow instance state so My Space can resume active work, not only show saved items and learning.
  • Support copy/export and reviewer handoff from the first build slice.

Workstream 4 — Trust, data-boundary, and review layer

  • Add a reusable trust panel component to every workflow and key tool card.
  • Show what data is allowed, what must not be used, whether review is mandatory, and who owns the asset.
  • Add explicit review checkpoints for client-facing or executive-facing outputs.

Workstream 5 — Telemetry and feedback

  • Instrument task start, workflow launch, step completion, tool launch, save/resume, review complete, and feedback pulse events.
  • Track hero-workflow performance separately from general portal browsing.
  • Use telemetry to decide whether to deepen agentic behavior or fix routing/content first.

Workstream 6 — Content operations and owner model

  • Name one workflow owner and one content owner per hero workflow.
  • Require “last validated,” “best for,” “not for,” and “review required” fields on all linked assets.
  • Archive or de-rank stale assets instead of letting search keep surfacing them.
Smallest front-end change list

UI and interaction work

  • Add hero-workflow cards and a task-intake panel on the home page.
  • Replace flat result lists with grouped result blocks and a “recommended workflow” hero card.
  • Create a shared ResultCard v2 with best-for, not-for, input-needed, output-type, trust badge, and launch CTA.
  • Add a WorkflowWorkspace shell: stepper, linked assets, trust panel, output tabs, save/resume, reviewer handoff.
  • Add a lightweight “why recommended” explanation box wherever ranking/personalization affects order.
  • Make My Space show active workflow instances, not just saved items.
Smallest back-end change list

Service and persistence work

  • Create workflow_definition, workflow_step, workflow_asset_link, workflow_instance, workflow_instance_step, review_gate, and event_log entities.
  • Add a task-intake endpoint that maps a query + context into one recommended workflow and a ranked asset set.
  • Add workflow instance create / update / resume endpoints.
  • Add a review-gate endpoint and status model.
  • Add an event capture endpoint for telemetry and feedback pulses.
  • Keep the ranking layer simple and inspectable at first: rule-based + metadata weighting + search score.
Content and operations

Non-code work that is still essential

  • For each hero workflow, prepare: example inputs, output skeletons, sample prompts, do-not-use notes, trust guidance, and reviewer checklist.
  • Curate the exact prompt pack and example outputs that will be shown inside the workflow shell.
  • Normalize metadata on linked assets so they can be ranked and reused consistently.
  • Create one concise learning card per workflow step for new users.
Release quality

Testing and validation gates

  • Run click-path QA for home → task router → workflow launch → save/resume → review → export.
  • Test data-boundary warnings and review-required states explicitly.
  • Run short walkthroughs with representative roles from at least three CoEs before general release.
  • Verify telemetry for every major workflow event before rollout.
  • Have content owners sign off on linked assets and example outputs.
Implementation principle: use the first slice to establish the reusable product pattern — task intake, grouped results, workflow shell, trust panel, save/resume, review gate, telemetry. Once these exist once, the next workflow becomes much cheaper.

Technical blueprint

The aim is to build a workflow layer on top of the current portal and content estate, not to replace the entire stack. The architecture should therefore be config-driven, adapter-friendly, and explicit about boundaries.

Target architecture

Experience layer

Home task-intake, hero workflow launchers, grouped search results, workflow workspace, My Space resume, review UI.

Workflow services

Task router, workflow registry, workflow instance manager, review-gate service, feedback pulse service.

Content & search

Asset index, metadata store, prompt packs, examples, policies, freshness and owner data, grouped search results.

Adapters

Copilot, Proposal Builder, Create a Presentation, Generate Insights, SharePoint/source assets, optional later agent adapters.

State & telemetry

Workflow instance state, step events, trust events, review events, usage metrics, content freshness signals.

Why this architecture is the right one

  • It separates workflow logic from page logic.
  • It keeps the first slice compatible with current tools and content sources.
  • It allows later agentic behavior to plug into an existing workflow shell rather than forcing a second redesign.
  • It makes ranking and routing inspectable, which matters when users need to trust why something was recommended.

Simple ranking formula

score = (0.35 * intent_match) + (0.15 * role_fit) + (0.15 * bu_fit) + (0.10 * freshness) + (0.10 * trust_fit) + (0.10 * proven_success) + (0.05 * setup_speed)

Example event payload

{ "event": "workflow_step_completed", "workflow_id": "wf_meeting_actions", "instance_id": "inst_8421", "step": "extract_actions", "role": "Analyst", "bu": "TSA", "data_boundary": "accenture_internal", "review_required": false, "timestamp": "2026-04-23T10:15:00Z" }

Suggested product entities

  • workflow_definition — the reusable blueprint for each workflow
  • workflow_step — ordered steps with step types, trust rules, and linked assets
  • workflow_asset_link — tools, prompts, examples, training, policies, and related patterns
  • workflow_instance — a user’s live run of a workflow
  • workflow_instance_step — saved progress and outputs by step
  • review_gate — explicit review requirement and status
  • event_log — telemetry, feedback, trust, and review events

Boundary-aware routing logic

  • Every workflow launch should require a data-boundary choice or infer one from the entry point.
  • Assets should declare whether they are safe for Accenture-only, client-permitted, or restricted contexts.
  • When a user is in a restricted context, the portal should route to the safe subset of steps and tools, not just show a generic warning.
  • This matters because the survey’s top adoption barrier is not model quality — it is boundary safety.

Practical component map

  • TaskIntakePanel — collects intent, deliverable type, and boundary context
  • ResultGroup — shows recommended workflow, tools, prompts, examples, and learning in one response frame
  • WorkflowWorkspace — shared shell for all workflow runs
  • TrustPanel — data rules, owner, review requirement, and limitations
  • ReviewGateCard — reviewer assignment and completion state
  • ResumeCard — active workflow instances in My Space
If the current portal codebase is indeed a React / Next-style app, these can be introduced as route-level and shared components without a full rewrite.

Minimum service / API surface

POST /api/task-intake GET /api/workflows/:id POST /api/workflows/:id/launch GET /api/instances/:id PATCH /api/instances/:id POST /api/reviews/:id/complete POST /api/events
Keep the initial service layer narrow. The goal is to support routing, save/resume, review, and telemetry for the hero flows — not to introduce a generic BPM platform in the first concentration bet.

Risks, metrics, and MD Q&A

The strongest workflow-first plans are explicit about what can go wrong, how success will be measured, and how to answer the predictable “why this and not that?” questions from leadership.

Key risks and the corresponding design response

Do not start with broad free-form agents

The biggest survey barrier is data restriction, not lack of autonomy. Unbounded agents will amplify trust and boundary issues before the core flows are stable.

Do not spread the first slice across too many workflows

The point of a hero-workflow strategy is concentrated quality. If every flow gets a shallow treatment, none becomes the new default way of working.

Do not make new platform procurement a hard dependency

Survey respondents clearly want Claude and richer agent tooling, but the first build should still work on top of the assets and tools already present.

Do not hide review and data-boundary rules

Lack of trust and client restrictions are already visible barriers. If the UX makes these rules invisible, adoption will stay shallow or unsafe.

Likely MD questions — and the crisp answer

Why not build a full agentic search bar immediately?

Because the survey shows the bigger blockers are data boundaries, trust, and unclear use cases. A broad autonomous layer would magnify those weaknesses. A hero-workflow shell solves the real problem first.

Why these four workflows?

They best balance survey pull, cross-CoE relevance, current portal seed assets, and realistic implementation effort. They also let the product prove routing, save/resume, trust, and review patterns quickly.

Why not just add more tools?

Tool volume is not the problem. The portal already has approved tools, and respondents are already using several external tools. The gap is routing people into the right workflow with the right guardrails.

What if leadership wants “agentic” visible now?

Make agentic visible inside the selected workflows as bounded planning and safe step suggestions, not as a free-form system-wide agent.

How will we know this worked?

By measuring workflow start rate, step completion, repeat usage, review completion, time to first useful output, and the share of traffic that converts from browsing into active work.

Bottom line: this route keeps the product ambitious but believable. It shows a near-term build slice, a reusable product pattern, a safe path to agentic behavior, and a clear way to explain to leadership why concentrating on four flows is stronger than broadening the catalog again.
